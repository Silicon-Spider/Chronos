## Bucket edges

**Data.** The 479 complete records of the ND8 start games: 15,387 distinct turn starts and 2,496,465 relations. `chronos-relhist` produced the relations and `tools/relations_g0.py` analysed them.

**What counts as a relation:**

- A relation is a pseudo-legal capture geometry from a piece on a head board onto a piece, enemy or own (a defence).
- Both colours count, from every head.
- The side not to move on a head is read as check status (R1) reads it: through an unchanged copy of the head one half-turn on, applied to every head ("semantics B").
- Under the turn-aware rule, the opponent's pieces on its own heads are read on that same passed multiverse.
- X-rays and pins are left out.

Shares by family: same board 37.0%, timeline only 13.3%, time only 30.4%, both 19.3%.

| Family | Distance | Histogram (start games) | Edges | Share per class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Timeline only (ΔT = 0) | \|ΔL\| timelines | 1: 273,825; 2: 53,760; 3: 3,163; 4: 527; 5+: 111 | **1, 2, 3+** | 82.6%, 16.2%, 1.1% |
| Time only (ΔL = 0) | \|ΔT\| whole turns | 1: 536,837; 2: 182,224; 3: 23,569; 4: 10,153; 5+: 6,760 | **1, 2, 3+** | 70.7%, 24.0%, 5.3% |
| Both | max(\|ΔL\|, \|ΔT\|) | 1: 418,503; 2: 61,735; 3: 1,878; 4+: 134 | **1, 2+** | 86.8%, 13.2% |

- **The rule.** Within each family, take the contiguous split into the spec's number of classes whose counts have the highest entropy, that is, the most even split the histogram allows. It returns the spec's own edges. The same rule run on half-turn distances returns them too, so the data back these edges in either unit.
- **ΔT is measured in whole turns along the move, not half-turns.** Pieces move in whole turns.
  - Read on the real multiverse, every relation's half-turn ΔT is even: twice the turns.
  - Read through a passed copy, most cross-board targets get an odd half-turn ΔT (757,479 relations).
  - Half-turn classes would therefore sort relations by how they were read, not by how far they reach. Under the spec's literal half-turn reading:
    - 124,625 relations move from "timeline only" to "both";
    - the classes would hold 170,237 / 34,427 / 2,097 timeline only, 271,597 / 265,240 / 222,706 time only, and 249,086 / 357,789 both.
- **Time-only relations always point back in time.** There are no exceptions among 759,543, since a head has nothing ahead of it on its own timeline.
- **Past-self defences.** The histograms include 143,764 same-kind, same-square defences onto the attacker's own timeline's past boards, usually the piece's own earlier self (18.9% of time-only relations). The edges don't change if they are left out (proposal 10).

**Δt to the present** (NI6) is the head board's half-turn minus the present, over all 25,202 head boards of the start games' positions.

| Bucket | <0 | 0 | 1-2 | 3-5 | 6-10 | 11+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Head boards | 611 | 17,611 | 1,663 | 1,904 | 1,799 | 1,614 |
| Share | 2.4% | 69.9% | 6.6% | 7.6% | 7.1% | 6.4% |

- Every mandatory head is at 0. The opponent's heads sit at odd Δt and the mover's optional heads at even Δt, and only inactive heads fall behind the present.
- Negative values, an inactive head behind the present, keep one bucket of their own. The other five come from the same maximum-entropy rule over 0, 1, 2 and so on.
- The last edge is a near tie: 6-9 and 10+ is less even by only 0.00025 nats, so more data could move it.
- The unit is half-turns, because who moves next on a board is its own placement feature.

**Where the impossible rows come from.**

- **32,256 of the 32,832 come from the rules engine itself (P2).** `chronos-relhist --probe` places each kind of each colour on every square of an 8x8 board and of a 9-timeline multiverse. The copied movegen then says which squares and which (ΔL, ΔT) it reaches.
- **The other 576 come from the promotion-rank rule in the analysis script.** Moving that rule into the probe, by letting the engine show that every pawn or brawn move onto the last rank promotes, is a small Phase 1 task.
- **Check:** no row the probe calls impossible fires in the 2.5 million relations of the start games.

What the probe reaches, by kind:

| Kind | Reaches |
| --- | --- |
| Pawn | same board, both 1 |
| Knight | same board, timeline and time 1-2, both 2+ (never both 1) |
| Rook | every class except both |
| King, common king, brawn | same board, timeline 1, time 1, both 1 |
| Unicorn | every class except same board |
| Dragon | both 1 and both 2+ only |
| Bishop, queen, princess, royal queen | every class |

**The symmetric drop isn't free.** The index holds only the victim's square. Each kept white-attacker row records the black piece's square; the dropped row recorded the white piece's. So the pair still shows, but the second square is lost. En passant isn't mutual either (14 captures in the start games). In the start games, 6,671 relations fall on the 206 dropped rows that fire.

**What isn't trimmed, and why:**

- **Own pairs of one kind.** They are mutual for every kind except pawns and brawns, which defend only forward. But with no attacker square in the index, there is nothing to choose one of the two by. Stockfish's threat inputs (`full_threats.cpp`, linked from spec §13) can break the tie by square order because they carry both squares.
- **Cross-board rows.** They mix head-to-head pairs, which can be mutual, with attacks onto history boards, which never are, so no whole row is redundant.
- **Zones.** Every 2x2 zone is reachable by every kind in every class it reaches at all, once the attacker's square is unknown.
- **King victims stay.** Attacks on royals include every check, and checks decide 5D games. They also include some threats R1 doesn't count as check (proposal 9). Stockfish's threat inputs give the king no victim features.

Only 9,578 live rows fire in the start games, all among the six gen-1 pieces. The other six kinds can be trimmed only by geometry, which is what the probe does.

## t0database split (ND8)

`tools/split_t0db.py`, with seed 20261108, writes `data/splits/t0database-nd8.tsv`: all 761 games, each with its set, cluster and status.

- **A game is a cluster** of rows that record the same game. Rows join a cluster in three ways:
  - their move text is identical once clocks are removed (12 links);
  - two complete records have equal turn-start sequences (22 links);
  - a shorter or refused record's turn starts open exactly one game, and that game either names the same players on the same day or shares at least 20 turn starts with it (3 links: 19~151, 156~170, and 462~506, a truncated copy).

  Eight short records open several games and stay apart. That gives 736 clusters, and no cluster is split.
- **Assignment.** Clusters are ordered by SHA-256 of the seed and their smallest GameNum. They fill the start set while it holds fewer than two thirds of the games (507).

| Set | Games | Usable | Duplicate | Refused |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Start | 507 | 465 | 14 | 28 |
| Held out | 254 | 227 | 10 | 17 |
| **All** | **761** | **692** | **24** | **45** |

- **Usable** reproduces Temporal-5D's 692: the records that replay to the end in parse mode (716), less those that equal or are a prefix of a longer record.
- **Refused records:**
  - 13 end in an unfinished final action;
  - 19 replay as illegal moves because their set-ups differ from Turn Zero (15 Defended Pawn, 2 Two Timelines, 2 unknown set-ups);
  - 13 are unreadable (10 doubled-file pawn captures, 3 notation faults).
- **The held-out third stays unseen.** The bucket edges were cut from the start games only. One relation-semantics cross-check (proposal 9) was also run over all games, as a check of the tool, not an input to any cut.

## §3.3 review

The review was made against the pinned code. Each finding was drafted by one analyst, critiqued by two adversarial reviewers, then revised. The findings give expected gain, risk and the tests affected, as §3.3 asks. No change has been made.

Every gain is an estimate. The counts come from tracing the code; the times and percentages also assume unmeasured per-operation costs, such as 2-6 ns per locked update and 20-80 ns per heap allocation. No gain was timed.

**The three with the biggest expected gain are items 6, 1 and 2.**

- **Item 6 comes first** because it removes the throwaway copies that dominate cut and quiescence nodes, and most of item 1's traffic comes from those same copies.
- **Item 1 comes next.** It is worth 10-40% of node time on Windows before item 6, and its per-thread roots are needed for Lazy SMP anyway.
- **Item 2** is the condition for NN12: eval at 50% or less of node time is out of reach without incremental per-board state.
- **After item 6,** items 1, 2 and 3 can't be told apart at this confidence.

**1. Board storage: per-thread pools (NB2).** Copied files changed: `board.cpp`, `multiverse.hpp` and `multiverse.cpp`, `fen.cpp`; `board.hpp` too for the last step.

- *Finding.* `BoardRef` and `TimelineRef` are `std::shared_ptr`. On MinGW every reference-count increment is a locked instruction, even in one thread. One physical sub-move on a copy costs 2T + 2t + 2c locked updates and 3 heap allocations. T is the timeline count, t the touched timeline's tail length, and c is 1 once that timeline has a full chunk; at T = 5, t = 8.5 and c = 1 that is 29 updates.
- *Proposed.* In order:
  - a Chronos precondition that edits no copied code: each search thread builds its own root by replaying the record;
  - pool allocation behind the unchanged handle types (`allocate_shared` over per-thread slabs);
  - non-atomic handles, only if pooling leaves 3% or more of node time.
- *Expected gain.* 10-40% of single-thread node time on Windows/MinGW before item 6 (low confidence; 5-60% can't be ruled out), and 2-15% after it. On Linux, single-thread counts aren't locked, so the gain there is mostly allocation. Per-thread roots also remove contention on a shared root under Lazy SMP.
- *Risk.* Medium. Lifetime or thread-confinement mistakes show up as silent corruption, and no buildable copied test starts a thread or injects `bad_alloc`.
- *Tests affected.* These must pass unchanged: `test_board`, `test_multiverse`, `test_apply`, `test_random_play`, `test_zobrist`, `test_fen` and `test_pgn_*`, `test_attacks`, `test_turngen`, and `test_detectors`, whose pinned hash `0x5366691BCAF734AB` must not change. New tests: the suite under a pool scope, steady-state allocation counts, parity of fixed-node runs, fault injection, and ThreadSanitizer on Linux.

**2. Accumulator beside each board, built lazily (NN9).** Copied files changed: none.

- *Proposed.* All new Chronos code, and `BoardBuilder` needs no change list. A board's state is built at first use from its lineage parent (the board one half-turn back on its timeline, or its branch origin), by diffing the two boards' 64-byte square codes.
- *Expected gain.* An eval builds 1-2 new board states at about 0.2-0.6 µs each, instead of refreshing up to 5T boards at 1-5 µs each. That is about 10-50 times less board-state work at one timeline, and more with more timelines.
  - The gain comes from keeping incremental per-board state (NN9). Deferring it saves under 1% over building it when the search adopts a child.
  - Against building state eagerly at publish, it saves one state per non-branching candidate per cut node, about 4-23 µs at the median position, and less once item 6 stops the generator applying every candidate.
- *Risk.* Low to medium. A wrong cache key gives silently wrong evals. The guards are a key of content hash, board size and net; NM2 at every node; and NE4.
- *Tests affected.* No copied test changes. `test_board`, `test_apply`, `test_special_moves`, `test_multiverse`, `test_zobrist`, `test_random_play`, `test_check` and `test_turngen` guard the parent and diff rules. New tests: board state equals a refresh at every node, path independence, and keys that separate sizes and nets.

**3. Cached embedding (NN2, NN13).** Copied files changed: none.

- *Proposed.* One record per board: a hot 128-byte part (int8 embedding 2 × 32 and int32 material 2 × 5) and the 1 KB accumulator. It is kept per thread, outside `t5d::Board`. A board equal to its parent, such as a passed or null-move board, shares the parent's record.
- *Expected gain.* The 256 → 32 layer (16,384 int8 MACs) is skipped for every board read except new ones.
  - Head-and-embedding MACs fall 1.6× at one timeline, 2.2× at two, 3.3× at four, and about 3.3-7× at 8-24.
  - That is about 0.1-0.5 µs per eval at one timeline and 1-14 µs at 8-24, from instruction counts for a kernel that doesn't exist yet.
  - At one or two timelines, most of the saving comes from NI1's past boards.
- *Risk.* Medium. A stale record is reused by every eval that reads it, slot lifetimes have to be right, and a content cache may cost more than it saves.
- *Tests affected.* No copied file changes. New: embedding and material sums equal from-scratch values at every node, in the four gen-1 variants and one 5x5 variant.

**4. Attack indices from movegen (NI3, NI4).** Copied files changed: none, as re-scoped.

- *Finding.* As written, this saves about nothing.
  - Putting net indices into movegen would move G0's edges, zones and mirroring, and arms NX4, NX12, NX13 and NX17, into copied rules code (against P2 and NF6). The whole copied suite would then re-run for every change to the net.
  - The turn generator's own pass reads one colour, under relaxed rules.
- *Proposed.* Re-scope it as a Chronos-owned relation-fact source over the unchanged API:
  - captures from moves;
  - defences from `RelationKinds{defence}`;
  - the side not to move through R1's passed copy.

  `chronos-relhist` already implements these semantics. The index function stays separate, in the featuriser.
- *Expected gain.* About 0 in node time, by construction. The value is a complete from-scratch fact oracle for NM2 and NM3, without editing copied code. Emitting facts from the generator would save at most about 1.2-3.2 µs per expanded node, under 10% of the generator's set-up, and always-on emission would cost more.
- *Risk.* Low for the re-scoped form. Medium for the reading of the side not to move, which needs a ruling (ruling 3).
- *Tests affected.* `test_state` (`state_moves_of_either_colour_for_the_check_ruling`), `test_check` (`check_royal_captures_equal_the_move_generators`) and `test_attacks`. New: `test_relations`, which checks that relations never change the moves and appear once each.

**5. Attack maps for every attack (NN3).** Copied files changed: `attacks.cpp` and `attacks.hpp`, if built.

- *Finding.* The royal-attack maps' update rule carries over to every attack, but the gain is capped by the heads that survive a turn.
  - On t0database positions, the ceiling is about 0.9-1.2× under 50 boards (88.6% of turns), 1.4-2× at 50-99 and 1.8-2.6× at 100-199.
  - As copied, with stop records, sorting, allocations and reference counts, the maps are slower than a plain scan nearly everywhere in that range.
- *Proposed.* First build the from-scratch scan of every head, which NL1, NM3 and NM2 need regardless, and count how often heads survive in Chronos's own trees. Extend the maps to all victims, both colours and every head only if those counts say they pay.
- *Expected gain.* Low confidence.
  - Pooled maps with stop-free records on mandatory heads approach the ceiling: about 0.8-1.1× under 50 boards, 1.3-1.8× at 50-99 and 1.6-2.3× at 100-199.
  - Re-extending lines where every head is played gives 0.6-1.6×.
- *Risk.* Medium. The convention that keeps updates exact drops the non-mover's cross-board attacks on the mover's present boards. Those are R1's checks, which §7.6 says decide games.
- *Tests affected.* `test_attacks` (4 tests, 5 with `--long`), `test_check`, `test_movegen` and `test_movegen_timelines`. New: NM2 tests of the maps against a scratch scan under every ruleset.

**6. Turn generator: integer scores, per-thread pooled memory, no heap allocation per node (NT2).** Copied files changed: `state.cpp` and `hypercuboid.cpp`.

- *Finding.* At construction, the generator applies every non-branching pseudo-legal candidate to a fresh copy of the turn start, only to read which squares change. Each emitted one-move turn then costs five position copies and 6-9 other heap blocks.
  - The baseline gives the total: 16.6 µs to the first legal turn under 50 boards, about 10 movegen calls' time, and 0.7-15.5 ms on synthetic 100-600-board positions.
  - How much of that total is construction's copies hasn't been measured; item 6's bench will split it.
- *Proposed.* Each step is followed by the suite, the gate, both pins and the bench.
  - First, extend `chronos-bench`'s turn-generation section with allocation counts and a split between construction and `next()`, and add a pinned generator hash. The detector pin never runs the best-first path.
  - 6a: allocation-free change sets. `apply()`'s board edits move into a helper on stack `BoardBuilder`s, without publishing.
  - 6b: per-thread pooled working memory.
  - 6c: integer scores.
  - 6d: prebuilt-board apply, together with item 2.
- *Expected gain.* 90-95% fewer heap allocations per node and 65-93% fewer reference-count updates, at every kind of node (a static tally, ±30%). Construction, most of the work at cut and quiescence nodes, loses its copies. Integer scores bring exactness, not speed.
- *Risk.* Medium. This code is the search's only source of legality (NT2, NF13). A bug means an illegal turn, or a missed legal one and so possibly a false mate. Only `test_turngen` runs the best-first path, and the generator's memory stop has no copied test.
- *Tests affected.* `test_turngen` (11 tests, 13 with `--long`), `test_detectors` (pinned hash included), `test_check`, `test_attacks`, `test_gate`, `test_gate_exe`, `test_apply`, `test_state` and `test_special_moves`. New: the generator hash, "pooled equals fresh", and "helper change sets equal `apply()`'s diff".

**7. Move lists.** Copied files changed: `hypercuboid.cpp`, a few lines.

- *Finding.* "Fixed-size" can't hold literally: one t0database position has at least 626 moves. Read it as buffers reserved once and reused, with growth as the overflow path.
- *Proposed.* 7a: reserve and reuse within one generator (the candidate list, `moves_`, `scratch_`, and `realise_free`'s sequence as a member). The later steps fold into item 6.
- *Expected gain.* Small. 7a with the later pooling (7c) saves about 1-3% of node time on glibc and 1.5-5% on MSYS2/UCRT at cut nodes under 50 boards. It saves less at all-nodes, under 1% at 100+ boards, and nothing for bare movegen. 7a alone gives most of it. Its value is as item 6's first mechanical step, with an exact allocation-count test.
- *Risk.* Low.
- *Tests affected.* `test_turngen`, `test_detectors` (pinned hash) and `test_gate`. New: a turn-generation pin and allocation counts.

**Suggested order:**

1. Baseline pins and benches first.
2. 7a.
3. Item 6.
4. Item 1, with per-thread roots first.
5. Items 2 and 3, with the net plumbing.
6. Item 4's fact source.
7. Item 5, last, and only if its counters say it pays.
